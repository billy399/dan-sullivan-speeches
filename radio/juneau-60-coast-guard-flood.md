Transcription notes:
- "Ahoy" is confirmed on both takes (re-checked in isolation). It is a charming opener for a Coast Guard guy; keep it.
- "how much **he** gets done": the recognizer heard "it" on both takes. Listen before cutting; if it is really "it," it still plays fine on radio.
- Pauses of roughly 1 s sit after "ships," "history," "Alaska," and "infrastructure." An editor can tighten these to pull the take down to about 27–28 s.

### Which take to use: **Take 2** (00:31.2 – 00:61.3)

- "homeported **here** in Alaska" lands better for a local buy and sets up the Storis line.
- "**other states** are jealous of how much he gets done" is a cleaner line than "the politicians are jealous."
- "including seven tours in Alaska" flows better than Take 1's clipped list.
- Only downside: "Dan Sullivan **has delivered**" is a hair softer than Take 1's "Dan Sullivan **delivers**" (Take 1, 00:07.8–00:09.0). Optional splice if the editor wants the punchier verb; the two takes are read at the same pace and level.

## 3. Fact backup for every claim in the tag

Research was done 2026-10-06. Several Alaska news and Senate sites blocked direct page reads from this environment, so quotes came through search extracts; confirm against the live pages before air.

| "New customers for local business" | Alaska Beacon: cutters "create demand for local shipyard work and stores." Juneau Assembly passed a resolution in June 2026 asking the Coast Guard to homeport a second cutter in Juneau. | Alaska Beacon (June 12, 2026); KTOO (June 22, 2026) |

| Testimonial: "four new icebreakers being homeported here in Alaska" | Storis (Juneau) plus three Arctic Security Cutters: two to Kodiak (from 2028), one to Seward (early 2030s). | ADN (June 11, 2026); Petroleum News (June 21, 2026) |

Caveats a fact-checker will raise:
- The Storis is temporarily based in Seattle and has visited Juneau once (Aug. 7–11, 2025). The Juneau base is not expected to be complete until at least 2029, per the Commandant (Feb. 2026). The copy says "homeported," which is the official designation, and avoids saying the ship is in Juneau now. Do not say "docked in Juneau" or "sailing from Juneau."
- Juneau has not broken ground on the $300M base yet. "New jobs" is forward-looking and the copy is phrased as what the buildup "means," not what has already been built.
- Housing is the main local concern. Sullivan himself called housing "a huge issue" (ADN, Apr. 17, 2026). Expect that rebuttal.
- Avoid the old "200 crew / 600 people" numbers.

### Glacial outburst flooding

| Claim in copy | Backup | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "glacial floods hit the Mendenhall Valley" | Record Suicide Basin outburst floods three years running: 14.97 ft (Aug. 2023), 15.99 ft with ~290 homes damaged (Aug. 2024), 16.65 ft (Aug. 2025). Two more outbursts in 2026 (Aug. 13 at 14.7 ft; Oct. 1–2 at 10.18 ft). | NOAA climate.gov; CBJ newsroom; NWS Juneau summary; KTOO |
| "stood with our neighbors" | Sullivan toured flood damage in Juneau Aug. 2024 ("We can't have it that Juneau has this problem every summer"); was in Juneau Aug. 10, 2025 and did a helicopter tour Aug. 16, 2025 with the Corps' Pacific Ocean Division commander; joint press briefing in Juneau with Assistant Secretary of the Army Adam Telle on Aug. 7, 2026. | thealaskastory.com; Sullivan on X (Aug. 12, 2025); Must Read Alaska; USACE release (Aug. 7, 2026) |
| "pushed the Army Corps of Engineers" | Sept. 27, 2024 delegation letter to the Corps' chief and the Assistant Secretary asking the Corps to prioritize a long-term solution; Nov. 19, 2024 letter to OMB; Dec. 3, 2024 meeting with Lt. Gen. Graham; GLOF study language in the American Relief Act (Dec. 2024), which CBJ credited as "championed by Senator Murkowski and Senator Sullivan"; secured Telle's pledge at his May 2025 confirmation hearing to fast-track a Juneau fix; Sept. 2025 and Feb. 2026 EPW hearings pressing the Corps; WRDA 2026 Sec. 1205 "Glacial Lake Outburst Flooding, Juneau, Alaska" directing the Corps to expedite the work (Senate committee-passed July 2026; House passed its WRDA Sept. 16, 2026; not yet law). | sullivan.senate.gov letters and releases; juneau.org newsroom (Dec. 2024, Mar. 26, 2026); govtrack S.4949 |
| "build the flood barriers" | Phase 1 (Mar.–May 2025): Corps supplied the HESCO barriers and about $4M in materials; 2.4 miles installed; 466 properties in the local improvement district. Phase 2 (Oct. 2025–July 2026): Corps agreed to pay 100% federal cost, about $19M, under Public Law 84-99; completed 2.72 miles of barriers, 1.85 miles of bank armoring and an earthen berm, rated for 63,500 cfs. | juneau.org HESCO Phase 1 and Phase 2 pages; KTOO (Oct. 31, 2025); USACE Alaska District release (July 24, 2026) |
| "now protect hundreds of Juneau homes" | 466 properties behind the Phase 1 levee; CBJ said Phase 1 covered about 90% of homes in the flood zone. Test: the record Aug. 2025 flood damaged about 47 homes versus about 290 in 2024. The Aug. 2026 flood affected 12 homes with about $591K total damage. | Juneau Empire; ADN (Aug. 19, 2025); Alaska Beacon (Aug. 13, 2025); KTOO (Aug. 19, 2026) |

Caveats a fact-checker will raise:
- The barriers were a joint effort: the City and Borough of Juneau, property owners (through the local improvement district), Tlingit & Haida, the State, and the Corps. The copy says Sullivan "pushed the Corps to build" them, not that he built them. Do not say "Dan Sullivan built the barriers."
- Senator Murkowski co-signed every delegation letter and, as an appropriator, secured monitoring money and requested study funding. The pieces that are specifically Sullivan's: the hearing commitments from Telle and Graham (he sits on EPW; she does not), the WRDA 2026 Juneau provision, and the 2025 and 2026 site visits with Corps leadership.
- No long-term fix is built yet. The Corps' lake-tap tunnel concept was chosen in Dec. 2025, re-opened in Feb. 2026, and the Corps now targets building a solution before the 2030 flood season. The copy stays on the barriers that exist today.
- No Sullivan-named dollar earmark for the barriers was found. Keep "pushed" rather than "funded."
- The Phase 1 levee is designed for 49,000 cfs and Phase 2 for 63,500 cfs. The 2025 flood caused seepage and storm-drain flooding behind the barriers even though they largely held, so "protect" is accurate but "flood-proof" would not be.
