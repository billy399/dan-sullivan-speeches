Topics: Due Process Protections Act, Brady evidence, prosecutorial misconduct, Ted Stevens case, criminal justice reform, bipartisan legislation

Tone: measured, legal, deeply personal (Ted Stevens connection)

Sullivan celebrated the unanimous passage of the bipartisan Due Process Protections Act, which he co-sponsored with Sen. Dick Durbin. The bill requires every federal judge to issue an explicit written order reminding prosecutors of their Brady evidence obligations at the start of every criminal case — closing the loophole that let the prosecutors in the Ted Stevens case escape accountability because no such standing order had been issued. Sullivan spoke with rare personal intensity about the Stevens prosecution, which he called a case of 'deliberate and systemic ethical violations.' The bill had the broadest bipartisan coalition imaginable: Graham, Durbin, Lee, Booker, Cornyn, Whitehouse, and Paul.

  • "The late great Senator Ted Stevens — federal prosecutors concealed numerous pieces of evidence that very likely could have resulted in his acquittal."
  • "The Justice Department special prosecutor found that lawyers had committed deliberate and systemic ethical violations by withholding critical evidence pointing to Senator Stevens' innocence."

Alaska: Strong Alaska connection: the bill was directly inspired by the prosecutorial misconduct in the Ted Stevens case — federal prosecutors withheld exculpatory (Brady) evidence, a conviction that changed Alaska's political landscape and cost Stevens his Senate seat.
